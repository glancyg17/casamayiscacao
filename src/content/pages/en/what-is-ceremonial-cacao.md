Ceremonial cacao is **100% pure cacao**: cacao beans that are fermented, dried, toasted and ground, with no sugar, no milk and no additives. It is prepared as a thick hot drink and sipped slowly, in a ceremony, in a circle, or in a quiet moment of your own.

## Cacao, cocoa and chocolate

They sound alike but are not the same. **Cacao** is the toasted, ground bean with everything it contains. **Cocoa** is usually a more processed powder, often with sugar or other ingredients. **Chocolate** bars are cacao mixed with sugar, milk and sometimes added fats.

## Where the custom comes from

Long before chocolate bars existed, Mesoamerican cultures, including the Maya and the Mexica, drank cacao. The word *cacao* comes from *kakaw*, a word from Mayan languages. They also used it as an offering and as currency.

Today there are many ways to approach cacao. At Casa Mayis we do it as a personal practice inspired by that tradition, with respect and without speaking for anyone.

## What makes it "ceremonial"

There is no official standard for the term, so here is what it means to us:

- **It is only cacao.** No sugar, milk or additives.
- **It is worked whole.** The cacao is toasted and ground complete, with its natural cocoa butter.
- **It is prepared with intention.** Warmed slowly, whisked, served and sipped without hurry.

Ours is from Tabasco and Mayis toasts and grinds it in Campeche. Read more in [Tabasco cacao](/en/tabasco-cacao/).

## How it is drunk

It is chopped or grated, dissolved in hot water without boiling, and whisked with a molinillo. The recipe is in [how to prepare ceremonial cacao](/en/how-to-prepare-ceremonial-cacao/).

## One important note

Cacao contains theobromine and a little caffeine, which are mild stimulants. If you are pregnant, have a health condition or take medication, check with your doctor first.
